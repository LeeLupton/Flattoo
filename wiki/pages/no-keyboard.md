The laptop is laid out for tapping: icons on the screen, the dock, the app grid, the power sheet, swipes on the touchpad, and [Ask Flattoo](ask-flattoo.html) as a list of things to tap. Where typing is still needed, there is a keyboard on the screen.

## The on-screen keyboard

Tap into any text box and a keyboard slides up from the bottom, phone style; it goes away when the box is done with. The **Keyboard** button at the right end of the top bar shows or hides it at any time. If it ever gets in the way, the same button puts it away.

## The lock screen and the login screen

Both have a keyboard button next to the password box. Tap it and type the password on the screen.

With a **fingerprint reader**, Lee can enrol a finger; from then on a touch on the reader unlocks the screen and the password is only the fallback.

## The passphrase at power-on

The passphrase typed on the dark Flattoo screen when the laptop starts is the one thing a screen keyboard cannot replace. Two ways around it:

- **Sleep instead of off.** Close the lid, open the lid. The passphrase is asked only after a full shut-down or restart, which the nightly updates never force.
- **A key on the keyring.** Lee can set up a small FIDO2 key: plug it in when the laptop starts, touch it, and the disk unlocks without typing. The passphrase keeps working for when the key is not around.

## Speaking instead of typing

When voice is switched on, Ask Flattoo has a **Say it** row: tap it, say what you need, and it shows what it heard before doing anything. Words are understood on the laptop; no recording leaves it.
