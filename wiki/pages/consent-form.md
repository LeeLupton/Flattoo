Tap **Consent form** on the client's card, or **Papers › Start a consent form**, or tell Flattoo *"consent form for Jon"*. Three screens, done with the client beside you:

1. **Who.** Full name, date of birth, phone, address, e-mail if they like, the tattoo and where it goes. If the date of birth makes them under 18 the form stops here — tattooing a minor is against the law in North Carolina, no exceptions, no parent's signature.
2. **The statements.** Read them together, tap each one that is true: 18 or older with ID shown; not under the influence; medical conditions, allergies, medications, pregnancy and skin conditions disclosed, skin at the site healthy; permanent and the result can vary; the design, spelling and placement approved; aftercare received; the risks accepted. The last one, photos for your portfolio, is optional — leave it unticked and the form says *not granted*.
3. **Sign.** The client signs with a finger in the box. *Clear* if it went wrong. **Save the form.**

The PDF lands in **Documents › Business › Consent** named by date and client, and *Print a copy* sends it to the printer if they want one. The client's details are remembered, so next time the form is mostly filled in.

## Why it matters

The state rules for tattooing (15A NCAC 18A .3206) say the artist keeps a record of every client — name, address, phone, date of birth, signature — for at least two years and shows it to the health department on request. This form is that record. Nothing in Money ever deletes one.

## The printer

It goes to the default printer; the stencil printer prints on A4 thermal paper fine. Lee can point it at another one.
